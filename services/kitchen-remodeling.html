Overview

What a kitchen remodel involves

Kitchen work covers a wide range. On one end is a remodel that keeps the existing footprint and replaces cabinetry, counters, appliances, and finishes. On the other is a layout change that relocates plumbing, gas, and electrical, opens a wall between the kitchen and living space, or absorbs an adjacent laundry or pantry area. The two projects look similar when they are finished, but they carry very different scopes, permit requirements, and schedules.

Much of the housing stock in Los Angeles and the San Fernando Valley predates current codes. Once cabinets and drywall come off, it is common to find galvanized supply lines, undersized electrical service, plaster over wood lath, non-vented range hoods, or framing that was modified during an earlier remodel. A&H plans for that discovery phase up front rather than treating it as a surprise, and any change to the agreed scope is documented in writing before the work proceeds.

Because the kitchen is usually the most-used room in the house, sequencing matters as much as selections. A&H sets the order of trades, the inspection points, and the decision deadlines that keep the space from sitting idle while a countertop template or a special-order cabinet is pending.

Typical scope

Every kitchen is scoped individually after a site visit. A full remodel commonly includes the following, and your written proposal states which of these apply to your project.

  • Protection and demolition — floor and doorway protection, dust containment, removal of existing cabinetry, counters, flooring, and finishes.
  • Framing and structural changes — wall modifications, header work for openings, and blocking for cabinetry, hoods, and appliances, engineered where the scope requires it.
  • Plumbing rough-in — supply and drain relocations for sink, dishwasher, and pot filler, plus gas line work for range and cooktop.
  • Electrical rough-in — circuits for appliances, counter-top receptacles, under-cabinet and recessed lighting, switching, and panel work where added load requires it.
  • Ventilation — range hood duct routing to exterior, make-up air where the hood size requires it.
  • Drywall, plaster, and prep — patching to match existing surfaces, new board where walls were opened, and level surfaces for cabinet and tile installation.
  • Cabinetry — layout verification, delivery coordination, install, and adjustment of doors, drawers, and hardware.
  • Countertops — template after cabinets are set, fabrication coordination, install, sink and cooktop cutouts.
  • Tile and backsplash — substrate prep, layout, setting, grout, and sealing.
  • Flooring — subfloor prep, transitions to adjoining rooms, and installation.
  • Paint and trim — base, casing, crown or light rail as designed, and finish paint.
  • Appliance set and final connections — placement, connection, and function check.
  • Permits and inspections — where the scope requires permits, A&H files and coordinates the inspections through completion.
  • Punch list and final walkthrough — a walk of the completed space together and close-out of the remaining items.

Our approach

How A&H runs a kitchen project

A kitchen remodel is a coordination problem before it is a construction problem. Cabinetry lead times, countertop templating, appliance dimensions, and inspection windows all have to line up, and a decision made late in one area moves everything downstream. A&H sets that order at the start of the project.

  • One point of contact. Ali runs the project. You are not routed between a salesperson, a designer, and a crew lead to get an answer.
  • Scope in writing before work starts. The proposal states what is included, what is carried as an allowance, and what is excluded, so pricing conversations happen before demolition rather than during it.
  • A selections schedule. Cabinetry, counters, tile, fixtures, and appliances each have a date by which the decision is needed to hold the sequence. You get that list early.
  • Appliance and cabinet dimensions verified first. Specifications are confirmed against the layout before cabinetry is ordered, because a fridge depth or a hood width found late is an expensive correction.
  • Site protection and daily cleanup. Containment at the work area, protected paths through the house, and a cleaned site at the end of each working day.
  • Licensed trades, coordinated by A&H. Plumbing, electrical, and specialty trades are scheduled and supervised as part of the project rather than left to the homeowner to manage.
  • Change orders in writing. If something behind the wall changes the scope, you get the description and the cost before the work continues.
  • Regular progress updates. You know what is happening in the coming week and what decisions are due.

Good project fit

A&H is likely a strong fit for your kitchen if:

  • You want a single contractor accountable for the whole scope rather than hiring trades separately.
  • The work touches more than one trade — for example cabinetry plus plumbing, electrical, and tile.
  • Your project is in North Hollywood, Los Angeles, or the surrounding areas.
  • You want a written scope and pricing before construction begins.
  • You are open to a site visit before receiving a number.

If you only need a single trade — for example a countertop swap with no other work — A&H will tell you that directly on the call rather than write a proposal you do not need.
